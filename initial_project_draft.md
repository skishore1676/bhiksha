## Architecture Design Document: Bhikshapatra (V2 Execution Core)
Vision: To build a highly parametric, ultra-low-latency event-driven execution engine capable of dynamically routing, executing, and managing 108 distinct options trading strategies generated by the Mala research agent.

Moving from the FSM-heavy V1 execution layer to the V2 "Bhikshapatra" architecture requires shifting from a static state machine to an Event-Driven Strategy Factory. The system will isolate broker execution from mathematical modeling, ensuring high resilience, minimal latency, and strict capital protection.

 Pillar 1: The Ingestion & Compute Pipeline (Data Prep & Setup)
Currently, your DataPoller fetches batch historical data every 5 minutes and patches the live price. For precision algorithmic strategies like the Market Impulse (which triggers exactly on a 1-minute bar close), batch polling introduces too much latency and alignment risk.

The Future State:

Streaming / Bar Aggregation: Transition to a WebSocket feed (or highly aggressive 1-minute API polling) that maintains an in-memory rolling buffer (e.g., 2 days of 1m bars, as recommended by your research).

The "Newton" Pipeline: Once a 1-minute bar officially closes, the ingestion engine passes the dataframe through your newton.transforms. It calculates the vma_10 and joins the 1-hour impulse_regime_1h down to the 1-minute timeframe.

Bar-Closed Events: The engine emits a BarClosedEvent(symbol="QQQ", timeframe="1m", data=latest_row).

Implementation: A dedicated asynchronous loop that fetches 1-minute bars (or consumes a WebSocket). It maintains a rolling 5-day deque of 1-minute bars for the sybmbols we have our strategy is tracking using some kind of config.yml file. 

Event Trigger: The exact millisecond a 1-minute bar closes, the aggregator pushes the data through the newton transforms and emits a BarClosedEvent onto the internal message bus.

3. The Strategy Factory (Parametric Instantiation)
When Bhikshapatra receives a SignalEvent, it must construct the rules of engagement dynamically.

Implementation: Build a StrategyFactory that reads the strategy_id. It instantiates a generic TradeStrategy base class loaded with the specific JSON parameters.

Interface: Every strategy class exposes a standard interface: evaluate_entry(bar), evaluate_exit(bar), and get_initial_stop_distance(). This means strategy #108 will use the exact same execution pipeline as strategy #1.

4. The V2 State Machine (Strategy-Agnostic FSM)
The V1 FSM has over 20 specific states (APPROACHING_TARGET_1, CANCELING_STOP_FOR_TARGET_1) which makes it brittle for new strategies. The V2 FSM will be stripped to its absolute minimum lifecycle states:

PENDING_ENTRY: Waiting for broker fill.

POSITION_OPEN: The FSM stops calculating targets. It simply passes every new BarClosedEvent or QuoteEvent to the injected Strategy object.

EVALUATING_EXIT: The Strategy object returned True for an exit condition (e.g., Close > VMA_10).

PENDING_EXIT: Waiting for broker square-off confirmation.

CLOSED: Terminal state.

## Part 3: Capital Protection & Robustness
When running 100+ strategies, portfolio-level risk management supersedes individual strategy logic. If a strategy goes rogue, the system must survive.

1. The Global Risk Governor (Circuit Breakers)
Before an OrderManager can place an entry order, the payload must pass through a global RiskGovernor class. This will evolve over time. 

Max Concurrent Trades: Cap the total number of open positions across all strategies.

Strategy Quotas: Prevent a single strategy from eating the entire buying power pool.

Daily Drawdown Limit: If the portfolio drops by $X or Y%, the Governor immediately switches to "Kill Switch" mode, rejecting all new entry signals and gracefully squaring off open positions.

2. Redundant Stop Management (Broker vs. Algorithmic)
Algorithmic Exits (Soft Stops): Strategies like Market Impulse use algorithmic triggers (e.g., reclaiming the VMA, or 15:55 ET time exits). These are evaluated on the closing tick of a bar.

Catastrophe Stops (Hard Stops): Because Public.com API outages or WebSocket disconnects can leave you blind, the RiskGovernor must always place a hard stop-loss on the broker's books the second a position opens. If the algorithm exits normally, it cancels the hard stop. If the internet goes down, the broker's hard stop protects the capital.

3. State Synchronization (The Reconciliation Loop)
We will retain the SimpleReconciliationWorker concept. Every 5 minutes, an independent thread fetches the broker's actual open positions and matches them against Bhikshapatra's internal FSM states. If a discrepancy is found (e.g., a strategy thinks it is flat, but the broker shows 5 open QQQ puts), it raises a critical alert and locks new entries.
